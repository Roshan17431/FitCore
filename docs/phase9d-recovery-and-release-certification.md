## Restore without credential revival or duplicate email

1. Fence old writers/workers and retire their workload leases through the external release authority. Remove customer ingress for the new SaaS only; never route it to old FitCore. Record the last trusted checkpoint and all externally observed acceptance/revocation events available after it.
2. Restore the DB in isolation, with startup traffic and email claims blocked. A copied identity row, role state or disposable marker is never approval for the restored resource. Obtain a new target/recovery epoch and fresh resource-bound permit from independent authority, held outside this DB/backups.
3. Place **all restored identities on a separate incident authentication hold**, then revoke/delete every restored browser session, rotate/reset accepted JWT signing generation or reject all old issuer/epoch tokens, invalidate pending verification/reset challenges and invitations, and cancel their obsolete email intents. Use explicit reviewed recovery operations, not silent application startup mutations. Do not weaken the primary enabled OWNER invariant or invent a global ADMIN recovery bypass. The hold is independent of users.enabled and cannot be removed by an ordinary app role update.
4. Treat restored account versions/password hashes/enabled flags/roles/email/verified state as historical. Old tokens can become valid again, and a stolen old password can obtain a **new** session under the fresh epoch. Epoch/key rotation fixes neither fresh-login authority nor restored demotion/disable/email-change state by itself. The conservative proposal assumes no independent live account-security journal: every restored identity remains authentication-held until an independent operator/security recovery process confirms its current rightful account control and allowed role/status/email, resets credentials through a reviewed recovery path, and issues a signed UUID/current-epoch account-reactivation receipt. Backup state or a message to the backup email alone is not independent evidence; missing evidence keeps the account held. Receipt issuance is separate from ordinary ADMIN/OWNER management, tied to the independently authorized recovery case and audited without exposing secrets. A future independently durable security journal may automate reconciliation only under separate approval. Test old-password fresh login, rolled-back enabled/role/email/verification state and already-running replica caches, not just old JWTs. An enabled/unverified trainer is no exception.
5. Quarantine all restored PENDING/PROCESSING and unresolved submission work. Merge independently retained provider receipts, webhook evidence and current suppression/bounce/complaint state. DB-restored first_attempt/receipt values may themselves be stale; where trustworthy external history is absent, do not replay. Verify frozen body/provider scope and original deadline before any permitted same-key retry. Beyond provider TTL or unknown outcome, require human resolution/fresh user request, never a new idempotency key for the old message.
6. Provider ACCEPTED/DELIVERED cannot be inferred from an old local row. Likewise an old PENDING row cannot prove the provider never accepted it. Keep receipt metadata independent enough to reconcile this uncertainty; apply idempotent inbox/suppression changes when provider webhooks are redelivered after restore.
7. Prove restored links/sessions and unreactivated **fresh logins** are rejected, keyring correct, tenant/ACL/schema contract intact and no email egress occurred in quarantine. Certify new artifacts/config/lease and reopen a limited monitored API cohort only after operator/security/data-loss sign-off. Reissue invitations/recovery instructions through fresh authorized flows as needed.
8. Resume the worker separately after a signed bounded resume decision, safe receipts/horizon/suppression state and available quotas. Reconciliation may mean deliberately not resending uncertain historical mail. Monitor acceptance uncertainty and duplicate-event counters; rollback must preserve newly committed SaaS data.

Recovery epoch/control records and required external receipt evidence need durable, independently backed-up integrity. If those systems are also lost, obtain an incident-specific recovery decision and keep sending/authorization closed; do not rebuild trust solely from the restored application DB.

## Monitoring, audit and incident operations

Collect structured low-cardinality reason codes, workload/artifact/epoch and trace IDs, request latency/error/rate denials, DB pool/locks/storage/backup age, outbox depth/oldest eligible age, lease backlog, acceptance uncertainty, webhook invalid/duplicate/delayed events, suppression and provider quota. Auth/admin/catalog/invitation actions have protected tenant/account UUID audit records. No raw credentials/JWTs/cookies/CSRF/link tokens/email body/address/raw IP or SQL bind/row details in application/driver/platform traces. Scope hashes are still personal-data pseudonyms requiring retention/access review.

Proposed starting alerts: any missing key/authentication/configuration fault or expired target lease is critical; eligible mail older than5min warns and reset-eligible work older than10min is critical; any unsafe uncertain-retry/restore attempt is critical; provider quota≥80% warns; missing cleanup heartbeat≥10min or backup age beyond approved RPO is critical. Confirm thresholds/on-call routing with measured SLO, not assumed production defaults. Provider outage can pause delivery while durably accepted eligible intents queue; limiter/DB/lease failure cannot fail open. Public recovery copy remains generic and must not promise delivery.

Document on-call, escalation, new-service traffic/sending kill switches, secrets compromise, queue drain/cancel, primary-owner account recovery, certified rollback and communications authority. Audit all operator actions and separate approver from executor. A new incident does not authorize access to old production.
